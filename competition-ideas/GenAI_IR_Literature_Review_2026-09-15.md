## 1. Scope and search approach

This targeted search identifies **12 starting references: four Taiwan-focused publications, one regional study covering Taiwan, and seven international references**. The Taiwan group includes three student studies and one practical report on university policy and library activities. The selected publications fall in 2023–2026. This does not establish that no relevant work appeared in 2021–2022.

Discovery used the **OpenAlex literature-search service**, supplemented by web searches for ERIC, PubMed, Taiwan's TOAJ platform, and publisher or institutional publication records. Bibliographic details and reported study characteristics were checked against available records. This is a targeted, non-exhaustive search, **not a systematic review or a full-text quality appraisal of every study**. Some assessments rely on abstracts.

The two OpenAlex discovery queries were:

```text
generative AI institutional research higher education
generative AI Taiwan higher education
```

Both used the publication-date filter `2021-09-15` through `2026-09-15`, inspecting the first 10 returned records per query. These broad searches returned off-topic results as well as useful leads; their total hit counts are not counts of eligible IR studies. Supplemental searches used combinations of:

```text
"generative AI" "institutional research"
"generative AI" "Taiwan" "higher education"
"校務研究" "生成式"
"generative AI" "academic advising"
"generative AI" "student feedback"
```

Title-specific searches and source checks then refined the selection. Taiwan inclusion is based on a reported Taiwan study setting or explicit inclusion of Taiwan university policies, rather than author affiliation alone. The nursing-college study is included as adjacent postsecondary evidence and is distinguished from university research.

Two perspectives organize the review:

- **GenAI as an institutional issue:** adoption, literacy, equity, governance, and learning outcomes.
- **GenAI as an IR tool:** analyzing student feedback, supporting advising, and explaining institutional data.

## 2. Taiwan research and regional research including Taiwan

### T1. University AI guidelines and library support in Taiwan

**柯俊如／Ko, Chun-Ru (2024). 臺灣頂尖大學的AI指引政策分析與政大圖書館的教學探索 / Analysis of AI Policy Guidelines at Taiwan’s Top Universities and Teaching Exploration at NCCU Libraries. _圖資與檔案學刊, 16_(2), 188–221 (issue 105). [Practical report / 實務工作報告]**

[TOAJ publication record](https://toaj.stpi.niar.org.tw/index/journal/volume/article/4b1141f99584be0601959353ed3808fa) · DOI: `10.6575/JILA.202412_(105).0005`

- **Evidence:** Analysis of AI guidelines at 16 Taiwan universities, alongside NCCU Library's AI literacy activities.
- **Finding:** Most policies emphasized faculty; concrete student guidance was limited, and only two universities mentioned libraries.
- **Limit:** Classified as a practical report in the [issue contents](https://toaj.stpi.niar.org.tw/index/journal/volume/4b1141f99584be06019592abd66c0724), rather than an impact evaluation. Assessment here uses the platform's bibliographic record, abstracts, and issue classification.
- **IR topic:** **校級 AI 政策與學生需求的落差** — Are university guidelines understandable, actionable, and supported by training?

This is the most direct Taiwan starting point for institutional policy and support questions in this shortlist.

### T2. Comparative university policies in Asia, including Taiwan

**Dai, Y., Lai, S., Lim, C. P., & Liu, A. (2025). University policies on generative AI in Asia: promising practices, gaps, and future directions. _Journal of Asian Public Policy, 18_(2), 260–281.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17516234.2024.2379070)

First published online August 8, 2024; the citation above uses the final 2025 issue year. These are two dates for the same publication, not separate studies.

- **Evidence:** Content analysis of policies at 30 Asian universities, including Taiwan institutions.
- **Focus:** Stakeholder guidance, assessment, instructor autonomy, and AI competencies.
- **Limit:** Policy documents do not establish actual implementation or effectiveness. Regional coverage should not be treated as a Taiwan-only sample.
- **IR topic:** **臺灣與亞洲大學 AI 治理比較** — Compare policy coverage and identify missing support mechanisms.

### T3. Taiwan college students' attitudes toward ChatGPT

**Yu, S.-C., Huang, Y.-M., & Wu, T.-T. (2024). Tool, Threat, Tutor, Talk, and Trend: College Students’ Attitudes toward ChatGPT. _Behavioral Sciences, 14_(9), 755.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs14090755) · [NCKU publication record](https://researchoutput.ncku.edu.tw/en/publications/tool-threat-tutor-talk-and-trend-college-students-attitudes-towar/)

- **Evidence:** Developed and evaluated a ChatGPT attitude scale with 516 Taiwan college students.
- **Finding:** Five attitude dimensions and some gender differences were identified.
- **Limit:** Attitudes are not demonstrated AI competence; subgroup differences do not establish their causes.
- **IR topic:** **學生 AI 使用態度與支持需求分群** — Which groups need different forms of assistance?

### T4. Evaluating AI literacy instruction in Taiwan nursing education

**Tseng, L.-P., Huang, L.-P., & Chen, W.-R. (2025). Exploring artificial intelligence literacy and the use of ChatGPT and copilot in instruction on nursing academic report writing. _Nurse Education Today, 147_, 106570.**

[PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39827788/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nedt.2025.106570)

- **Evidence:** 203 nursing students in southern Taiwan; an 18-week instructional intervention with a comparison group.
- **Finding:** Improved AI literacy and overall report performance were reported, alongside weaknesses in individualized nursing interventions.
- **Limit:** The setting was a nursing junior college, with allocation by existing classes. This is not evidence that a university-wide program would produce the same effects. Assessment here uses the abstract and available publisher description.
- **IR topic:** **AI 素養培訓成效評估** — Does training improve independently assessed work quality as well as confidence?

### T5. Trust, self-regulation, and student agency in Taiwan

**Chu, K.-M., & Chan, H.-C. (2026). Human–AI Co-intelligence in higher education: reframing student agency and learning strategies among Taiwanese undergraduates in the ChatGPT era. _Education and Information Technologies_.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-13993-3)

- **Evidence:** Reports mixed-methods research involving 320 Taiwan undergraduates, linking AI support, calibrated trust, self-regulation, and learning agency.
- **Limit:** Abstract-level assessment; reported statistical relationships should not be read as established causal effects. Full-text methodological appraisal remains necessary before adopting the model or measures.
- **IR topic:** **AI 依賴、信任與自主學習** — Who uses AI critically, and who delegates work without sufficient evaluation?

## 3. International research with direct IR applications

### I1. GenAI adoption within the IR profession

**Association for Institutional Research (2024). The Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Institutional Research/Effectiveness.**

[AIR research report](https://www.airweb.org/resources/research/community-surveys/generative-ai-in-ir-ie)

- **Evidence:** Professional survey with 469 responses and a 21% response rate.
- **Finding:** Most respondents rated their units' GenAI maturity as absent or reactive.
- **Limit:** Professional research report, not a journal experiment. Nonresponse and self-report limit generalization.
- **IR topic:** **IR 辦公室 AI 導入成熟度** — Staff skills, actual use, barriers, and organizational support.

### I2. University guidelines across institutional functions

**An, Y., Yu, J. H., & James, S. (2025). Investigating the higher education institutions’ guidelines and policies regarding the use of generative AI in teaching, learning, research, and administration. _International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education, 22_, Article 10.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-025-00507-3) · [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1460992)

- **Evidence:** 214 documents from 50 U.S. universities, analyzed using topic modeling, sentiment analysis, and qualitative coding.
- **Use:** A methodological precedent for comparing guidance across stakeholder groups.
- **Limit:** Written policy is not evidence of compliance or effectiveness. Automated text measures need contextual interpretation.
- **IR topic:** **校務 AI 政策盤點與缺口分析** — Compare guidance for students, faculty, researchers, and administrative staff.

### I3. Policy awareness and implementation

**Jiang, Y., Xie, L., & Cao, X. (2025). Exploring the Effectiveness of Institutional Policies and Regulations for Generative AI Usage in Higher Education. _Higher Education Quarterly, 79_(4), e70054.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/hequ.70054) · [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1487515)

- **Evidence:** Survey/interview research involving 124 undergraduates and seven faculty members at two U.S. universities.
- **Finding:** Institutional guidance was less clear than classroom policies; faculty reported training and implementation difficulties.
- **Limit:** A small institutional sample and observational evidence limit causal and broader claims about policy effectiveness.
- **IR topic:** **政策公告到實際理解的落差** — Measure policy awareness and students' ability to apply rules to realistic scenarios.

### I4. Summarizing course-evaluation comments with LLMs

**Zhang, M., Lindsay, E., Thorbensen, F. B., Poulsen, D. B., & Bjerva, J. (2024). Leveraging Large Language Models for Actionable Course Evaluation Student Feedback to Lecturers. In _Proceedings of the 52nd Annual Conference of the European Society for Engineering Education (SEFI)_ (pp. 1089–1098). Société européenne pour la formation des ingénieurs.**

[Published proceedings record](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/leveraging-large-language-models-for-actionable-course-evaluation/) · [arXiv author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01274)

The institutional record identifies a peer-reviewed proceedings article published in September 2024. Its citation gives Lindsay's initials as E.; the arXiv author list additionally includes the middle initial D. The arXiv identifier belongs to the author manuscript and is not presented as a separate study.

- **Evidence:** 742 student responses across 75 courses, used to generate summaries and suggested actions.
- **Use:** Demonstrates a feasible approach to processing course-evaluation text.
- **Limit:** Does not demonstrate subsequent improvements in teaching. Accuracy and usefulness must be checked in the intended local language and setting.
- **IR topic:** **教學意見文字分析助理** — Evaluate factual accuracy, omitted concerns, actionable suggestions, and staff time saved.

### I5. Major recommendations and academic advising

**Lekan, K., & Pardos, Z. A. (2025). AI-Augmented Advising: A Comparative Study of GPT-4 and Advisor-based Major Recommendations. _Journal of Learning Analytics, 12_(1), 110–128.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2025.8593)

- **Evidence:** Compared recommendations involving 33 students and 25 advisors.
- **Finding:** Advisors found explanations helpful but agreed with GPT's major recommendations only 33% of the time.
- **Limit:** Helpfulness of an explanation and agreement with a recommendation are different outcomes. The study does not establish improved graduation or career outcomes.
- **IR topic:** **跨域學習與學程導航** — Evaluate recommendation appropriateness and explanations separately.

### I6. Actual engagement with GenAI learning analytics

**Jin, F. J.-Y., Maheshi, B., Lai, W., Li, Y., Gasevic, D., Chen, G., Charwat, N., Chan, P. W. K., Martinez-Maldonado, R., Gašević, D., & Tsai, Y.-S. (2025). Students’ Perceptions of Generative AI-Powered Learning Analytics in the Feedback Process: A Feedback Literacy Perspective. _Journal of Learning Analytics, 12_(1), 152–168.**

[Publisher / DOI and complete author list](https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2025.8609)

- **Evidence:** Followed 18 students through introductory activities, actual use, and interviews.
- **Finding:** Positive initial impressions coexisted with modest subsequent engagement and mismatches with student expectations.
- **Limit:** A small exploratory study; attitudes observed during a demonstration are not evidence of sustained adoption or improved learning.
- **IR topic:** **AI 儀表板的實際使用成效** — Measure sustained use and decisions supported, beyond initial satisfaction.

### I7. Fairness of AI-writing detection

**Liang, W., Yuksekgonul, M., Mao, Y., Wu, E., & Zou, J. (2023). GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers. _Patterns, 4_(7), 100779.**

[Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)

- **Evidence:** Evaluation of seven detectors found substantial false positives for non-native English writing.
- **Limit:** Results concern the tested systems and datasets; they do not establish current performance for every detector or for Chinese writing. This is not a Taiwan sample.
- **IR topic:** **學術誠信程序與評量公平性** — Audit detection errors and how evidence is interpreted in institutional decisions.

## 4. Proposed competition research directions

The following are **proposed applications derived from the literature**, not findings already established at NCU. Required data access and participants have not been secured by this review.

| Suggested topic | Research question | Evidence and evaluation | Starting references |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **大學生成式 AI 政策的可理解性與落實程度** / Policy understanding and implementation | Do students understand what their university and courses permit? | Public policies, course syllabi where available, and scenario-based surveys; compare policy coverage with actual understanding. | T1, T2, I2, I3 |
| **AI 素養與資源取得差距** / AI literacy and access gaps | Do access, disciplinary background, and training predict the ability to verify AI output? | Survey plus performance tasks; distinguish attitudes, access, demonstrated competence, and learning outcomes. Use a suitable comparison design to evaluate training effects. | T1, T3, T4, T5 |
| **教學意見摘要與改善建議助理** / Course-evaluation analysis assistant | Can GenAI reduce analysis time while preserving accurate and minority viewpoints? | Authorized, de-identified comments; blind human comparison of accuracy, omissions, actionability, and time. Include a manual-analysis baseline. | I4 |
| **跨域學習與學程修課導航** / Cross-disciplinary course and program advising | Does document-grounded AI help students make appropriate course choices? | Course descriptions and program rules; assess prerequisite violations, citation accuracy, recommendation quality, and comprehension. Compare against existing information access or advising. | I5 |
| **AI 學習分析工具的採用與持續使用** / Adoption and sustained use of learning analytics | Why do students stop using tools they initially rate positively? | Usage logs and follow-up interviews; evaluate continued use and usefulness in real decisions. | I6 |

### Suggested priorities

1. **Policy understanding** is the strongest starting option using public documents. Measuring actual understanding additionally requires participants and appropriate research arrangements.
2. **AI literacy gaps** has useful Taiwan evidence and measurable outcomes, but an attitude-only survey would leave competence and learning effects unresolved.
3. **Course-evaluation analysis** is a concrete tool-building project if authorized comments can be obtained; the literature search does not establish their availability at NCU.
4. **Academic advising** is also well grounded, but recommendation quality, rule compliance, and source accuracy need separate validation.

For each project, identify the institutional user, the decision to support, the unit of analysis, a comparison baseline, and the evidence required to judge success. Distinguish GenAI's role in the proposed solution from the research design used to evaluate it.

## 5. Reading and evidence limits

- The list mixes journal research, a practical report, a published conference paper with an author-manuscript link, and a professional survey report; publication types are identified above.
- Policy coverage, self-reported attitudes, short-term performance, and sustained institutional outcomes are different kinds of evidence.
- Full texts, measurement instruments, sampling details, and local applicability should be reviewed before finalizing a study protocol.
- This review contains literature summaries and proposed topics, not NCU student-level data or permission to obtain or publish such data. Follow the [NCU IR Office application instructions](https://ir.ncu.edu.tw/p/412-1034-2329.php?Lang=zh-tw) for projects requiring institutional records.
- The search and source checks are dated September 15, 2026. Later readers should check for corrections, publication updates, and newer evidence.
